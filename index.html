01 / The challenge

Farmers need more than a watering schedule.

Soil appearance and fixed timings do not always reveal a plant's actual water requirements or internal water movement.

01 — LIMITED VISIBILITY

What is happening inside the plant?

Growers may not have real-time information about plant water uptake and early signs of water stress.

02 — INEFFICIENT IRRIGATION

Fixed schedules miss changing needs.

Watering by routine rather than measured conditions can lead to unnecessary watering or delayed irrigation.

03 — RESOURCE PRESSURE

Every resource matters.

Water and land constraints make precise crop management especially important in dense urban regions.

04 — ACCESSIBILITY

Advanced monitoring should be approachable.

Small-scale farmers and urban growers may not have access to advanced plant-monitoring technologies.

The opportunity: move from schedule-based watering to plant-informed irrigation.THAT'S WHERE UTKARSH BEGINS ↗

02 / Our solution

Understand the plant. Then respond.

UTKARSH combines plant-level sensing, soil monitoring and automated irrigation in one solar-powered concept.

〰️

Sap-flow-based plant monitoring

Adds a signal about water movement within the plant, alongside soil conditions.

💧

Need-based irrigation

Solar-supported operation

📊

03 / Technology

From sensor readings to irrigation action.

The current prototype architecture connects sensing, processing, indication and an automatic irrigation response.

STEP 01

Measure

Sap-flow-based sensing, soil moisture and other parameter sensors collect readings.

STEP 02

Process

An Arduino receives and processes the sensor data.

STEP 03

Evaluate

Readings are compared with preset thresholds to indicate plant condition.

STEP 04

Display

An LED bar graph communicates the current health indication.

STEP 05

Respond

The irrigation mechanism activates when the system indicates a need.

STEP 06

Power

A solar panel with tracker supports the system's power supply.

Electronics note: The BC547 transistor is used in the prototype to amplify the xylem-related signal.

02 / Quantity rationale

Why are these quantities required?

The quantities are planned for one acre:

  • 25 soil moisture sensors: The acre is divided into 25 monitoring points, so soil moisture can be checked in different parts of the field.
  • 5 sap flow sensors: These monitor selected plants to understand their water requirements.
  • 5 solar tracking mechanisms: These are intended to improve solar energy collection at different points.
  • 1 central controller: Collects information from the sensors and coordinates the system.
  • 1 pump system: Supplies water when irrigation is required.

These are target estimates for the proposed one-acre configuration, not confirmed supplier quotations. Final costs may vary with component specifications, field conditions, taxes and installation requirements.
